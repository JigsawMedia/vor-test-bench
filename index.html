Who we are

BettingJesus is an odds comparison site. It exists to do two things and nothing else: show you the best available price on a bet you have already decided to make, and help you work out which sportsbook is worth an account. Everything on the site serves one of those two jobs or it does not get built.

How we make money

When you open an account through a link here, the bookmaker pays us a commission. It costs you nothing, and it does not decide which price we show as best, because the best price is whichever number is highest and we would be caught out inside a day if it were not.

What a commission does buy a book is a listing. So here is the line we hold: a book's money affects whether it is on the site, and never where it finishes in a price comparison. Those are different decisions and we keep them apart.

Where the prices come from

Prices arrive from a commercial odds feed and are written into the page itself rather than dropped in by a third party widget, so what you read is what we published. Every grid carries the time it was last updated and the site states how often it refreshes.

Best price means highest decimal return on that selection at the moment shown. No weighting, no sponsored ordering, no rounding in a book's favour. If the number has moved since you loaded the page, the timestamp will tell you.

We never publish an odds page without real prices on it. An empty grid with a heading over it is worse than no page.

How we test a bookmaker

Six steps, every time, before a review goes up:

  1. Open the account as an ordinary customer, with no affiliate fast-track.
  2. Fund it with our own money.
  3. Place real bets, including at least one on a market this site prices.
  4. Request a withdrawal and time it from request to cleared.
  5. Count the markets actually available on a named fixture, rather than quoting the book's own claim.
  6. Write it down with dates and keep the evidence.

A review that cannot name a tested account and a dated withdrawal does not publish.

What we will not do

  • Lead with tips. This is a price comparison site, not a tipster service, and previews exist to support odds pages rather than the other way round.
  • List a book in a market it cannot legally serve. Every listing carries its licence and the jurisdictions it accepts.
  • Publish a bonus we have not read in the operator's own terms. If we could not stand an offer up, the listing says so.
  • Run a house byline. Every page names the person who wrote it.
  • Let a commercial relationship move a price in a grid.

What the blocking tools do not cover

This part matters on a site like this one, and most sites like this one leave it out.

GAMSTOP only blocks operators licensed in Great Britain. It is the strongest single step available to a British bettor and it works well, but it is not a wall around the whole internet. An operator licensed somewhere else is not in the scheme and will not be blocked by it. We list six bookmakers on this site and not all six hold a Great Britain licence, so registering with GAMSTOP will not shut every door you can reach from these pages.

If you want the gap closed, pair GAMSTOP with a bank card gambling block, which most UK banks now offer in-app and which applies to the payment rather than the operator, and with device-level blocking software. Those three together cover far more than any one of them alone.

Deposit limits and time-outs set inside a single bookmaker account only apply to that account. They do nothing about the next account.

Signs worth taking seriously

None of these is a diagnosis. They are the things people describe afterwards, and they are specific enough to check honestly against your own last month.

  • You have bet to win back a loss, and then bet again to win back that one.
  • You know your biggest win from the last year and not your net position.
  • You have moved money from somewhere it was meant to be, or borrowed to fund a stake.
  • Someone has asked about it and you gave a number lower than the real one.
  • You are betting on competitions you do not follow and could not name five players in.
  • The bet matters more than the sport, and a result you have no money on is boring.
  • You have tried to stop for a set period and not managed it.

If you want a structured version of this rather than a list, GamCare and GambleAware both publish a short self-assessment.
